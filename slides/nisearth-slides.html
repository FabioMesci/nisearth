01 · The problemNISEarth

Disasters happen under clouds, smoke and darkness. Radar sees through them.

NISAR (NASA–ISRO) delivers free L‑band radar data of the whole planet every 12 days — day and night, through clouds and smoke, measuring ground motion to the centimetre. Yet an emergency manager still can't answer: What can it see? When will it pass? How far can I trust it? NISEarth answers those three questions, event by event.

01 · MEASURE

Our own pipeline, from raw NISAR

Backscatter change (HH/HV) for fire, flood and debris; chained InSAR for moving ground. Each 1–2 GB file is read remotely, only the area of interest — it runs on a laptop.

02 · VALIDATE

Checked against independent references

EFFIS perimeters, Copernicus EMS flood maps, NASA FIRMS hotspots, ERA5 rain, EMSC/USGS quakes — and NASA/JPL's own NISAR images, side by side with ours.

03 · DECIDE

From pixels to a risk map

People, buildings, roads, rail and hospitals under each footprint, combined into a per-cell risk index with cascade warnings — exported as CSV / GeoJSON.

A physical rule, not a black box: fire drops HV (leaves, branches) but not HH; harvest drops both. Without the HH test, precision falls from 89 % to 57 %.

The lesson is operational: radar constellations must be coordinated and tasked for emergencies. A gap is a result too.

04 · Risk managementNISEarth

A risk index that tells you where to look first

risk = (H × E × V)1/3

Per ~440 × 550 m cell, 0–1. Geometric mean as in the EC JRC's INFORM index: if any factor is zero, risk is zero.

Hazard

Radar footprint fused with EFFIS or Copernicus EMS, FIRMS intensity, InSAR rate and gradient.

Exposure

People (WorldPop), buildings, roads, rail, critical facilities (OSM), forest or cropland.

Vulnerability

Land-cover susceptibility, people under 5 or over 65, distance to hospital and fire station.

05 · From map to decisionNISEarth

Reducing future damage: one hazard prepares the next

Cascade warnings computed for every cell
Fire → rain & erosion
Niebla · 14,576 ha burned on slopes > 15° · 835 cells · 11.2 km of roads
Suggested actionStabilise slopes and drainage on those cells before the rains. The radar already saw the first rain hit the scar (7 Sep).
Subsidence → flooding
Mexico City · 35,399 ha sinking on flat ground · 1,908 km of roads · 56 km of rail
Suggested actionTarget drainage, pumping limits and building checks where sinking is fastest and most uneven.
Rain → slope failure
Rasuwa · 125.5 mm between passes · 28 cells · 34.7 km of roads
Suggested actionPreventive closures and slope inspection on the flagged stretches of the valley road.

Known limits

  • Only ~4 months of NISAR interferometry: rates are 108–120 day fits
  • Fire thresholds calibrated on the same event's EFFIS perimeter
  • Population is modelled (WorldPop 2020); flood depth is not observed
  • The index ranks cells — it is not a forecast or damage count

Next steps

  • Alert: FIRMS fire + next NISAR pass → scar map in hours
  • USGS ShakeMap for quakes; census data for social vulnerability
  • Combined risk for overlapping events
  • Longer NISAR series for multi-year subsidence
